# Theme Planner

The **Theme Planner** is a quick way to set your site's three main brand colors — **Primary**, **Secondary**, and **Accent** — and have the theme work out all the lighter shades for you. Find it under **Appearance → Theme Planner**.

Use it when you are setting up a new site, or any time you want to re-colour the whole site in one go. It is the fastest way to get a complete, coordinated palette in place; the *Site Colors* section of the Customizer is where you fine-tune individual areas afterwards.

## Why use it instead of Site Colors

Your palette is not just three colors. Primary and Secondary each have four steps — a full-strength shade and three progressively lighter tints — which the theme uses for backgrounds, hovers, and subtle panels throughout the site.

Setting all of those by hand is slow, and it is easy to end up with tints that do not quite belong together. The Theme Planner asks you for one color per family and generates the lighter steps from it, so the whole ramp stays consistent.

## Picking your colors

### 1. Choose your Primary color

At the top of the page, click the **Primary color** swatch and pick your main brand color. This is usually the strongest color in your church's logo.

As soon as you choose it, the four swatches beneath update to show the family the theme has generated: **Primary 100** (the color you picked, the strongest) followed by **Primary 200**, **300**, and **400**, each a little lighter than the last.

### 2. Use the suggestions for Secondary and Accent

Under the Primary swatches is a suggestion box. It offers two colors that go well with your Primary, and a **Suggestion type** dropdown that changes how they are chosen:

- **Split complementary** — the default. Two colors from the opposite side of the color wheel. Gives you strong contrast that still looks deliberate.
- **Triadic** — two colors evenly spaced around the wheel. Bold and colourful, good for a lively, family-focused site.
- **Analogous** — two close neighbours of your Primary. Calm and harmonious, good for a quieter, more traditional look.

The two small circles show which suggestion is headed for **Secondary** and which is headed for **Accent**. If you prefer them the other way round, click **Swap**.

When you are happy, click **Apply to Secondary & Accent**. The two suggestions drop into the real color pickers further down the page, and the Secondary family's lighter shades are generated at the same time.

### 3. Adjust by hand if you want

Nothing is locked in. You can ignore the suggestions entirely and set the **Secondary color** picker yourself — its lighter shades regenerate from whatever you choose. The **Accent** color is a single color with no lighter steps, so it is only ever the one swatch.

### 4. Save

Click **Save Colors** at the bottom. Your choices are applied across the site straight away, and the theme rebuilds its stylesheet in the background so visitors who are not logged in see the new colors immediately.

## What the Theme Planner does and does not change

It writes only the **Primary** family, the **Secondary** family, and the **Accent** color.

Everything else in your palette — your whites, off-whites, greys, blacks, and the accessibility outline colors — is left exactly as it is. So are all of the *assignments* that decide which color is used where: header background, menu links, hero text, card backgrounds, button colors, footer text, and so on.

That means you can safely re-run the Theme Planner on an established site to try a new brand color without losing any of the detailed choices you have already made. The areas of the site that were set to use your Primary color simply start showing the new Primary.

To change any of those other colors or assignments, go to **Appearance → Customize → Site Colors**.

> Tip: Export your settings from **Settings → Theme Settings** before experimenting with a new palette. If you do not like the result, importing that file puts your old colors straight back. See the *Theme Settings* page.

## A note on contrast

The Theme Planner picks colors that work well together visually, but it cannot tell whether text will be easy to read against them. After saving, click through a few pages of your site — especially your header, buttons, and any text sitting over a hero image — and check that everything is still comfortable to read. If something is hard to make out, adjust the individual assignment in *Site Colors* rather than changing the whole family.
