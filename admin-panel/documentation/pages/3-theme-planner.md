# Theme Planner

The **Theme Planner** is a shortcut for building your color palette. Instead of choosing eleven brand colors by hand in the Customizer, you pick **one** Primary color and the Planner works out the lighter shades for you — and suggests a Secondary and Accent color that go with it.

Find it under **Appearance → Theme Planner**.

## What it changes

The Planner fills in nine of the palette colors in **Appearance → Customize → Site Colors**:

- **Primary 100, 200, 300, and 400**
- **Secondary 100, 200, 300, and 400**
- **Accent**

It does not touch anything else. Your whites, greys, black, ADA outline, and error colors stay as they are, and so does every choice about *which* palette color each part of the site uses. Those assignments stay in the Customizer — see the *Customizer* page.

## Choosing your Primary color

At the top of the page, under **Primary**, use the **Primary color** picker to choose your main brand color. This becomes **Primary 100**, the darkest shade.

As soon as you pick it, the four swatches beneath update. **Primary 200**, **300**, and **400** are generated automatically by mixing your color with increasing amounts of white, so you get a consistent dark-to-light ramp without having to judge each step yourself.

> Tip: pick a fairly **dark** Primary color. The generated shades only go lighter, so a pale starting color leaves you with four shades that are all nearly white — and the theme uses Primary 100 for things like the header background and footer, where dark usually reads best.

## Using the suggestions

Below the Primary swatches is a suggestions card that proposes a **Secondary** and an **Accent** color based on color-wheel relationships to your Primary.

Pick a **Suggestion type**:

- **Split complementary** — two colors from the opposite side of the color wheel. Gives strong contrast while staying balanced. A safe default.
- **Triadic** — two colors evenly spaced around the wheel. Bolder and more colorful.
- **Analogous** — two neighbours of your Primary. Quiet and harmonious, good for a restrained look.

Two round previews show the result, labelled **→ Secondary** and **→ Accent**. Then:

- **Swap** — exchanges the two, so the color that was going to be your Secondary becomes the Accent and vice versa. Useful when one of the pair is the brighter of the two and you would rather it was the highlight.
- **Apply to Secondary & Accent** — copies the two previewed colors into the Secondary and Accent pickers further down the page, and regenerates the Secondary shade ramp.

Changing the suggestion type or clicking **Swap** only updates the previews. Nothing reaches your palette until you click **Apply to Secondary & Accent**, and nothing is saved until you click **Save Colors**.

## Secondary and Accent

You can also set these two by hand, whether or not you used the suggestions.

- **Secondary** works exactly like Primary: pick **Secondary color** and the 200, 300, and 400 shades are generated for you.
- **Accent** is a single color with no shade ramp, because the theme only ever uses one accent tone.

Applying a suggestion and then nudging the pickers is a perfectly normal way to work — use the suggestion as a starting point and adjust until it looks right.

## Saving

Click **Save Colors** at the bottom. The page reloads with a **"Colors saved."** confirmation, and the site's stylesheet is rebuilt straight away so logged-out visitors see the new colors immediately. You do not need to open the Customizer afterwards.

If you leave the page without saving, nothing changes.

## Before you start

The Planner **overwrites** your existing Primary, Secondary, and Accent colors, and there is no undo button. If you already have a palette you might want back, go to **Settings → Theme Settings** first and click **Export Theme Settings** to download a backup. See the *Theme Settings* page.

## Checking your work

A generated palette is a good starting point, not a guarantee. After saving, look at a few real pages and check that text is still comfortably readable against its background — particularly the header menu, the footer, and any card where text sits on top of an image. If something is hard to read, you can either adjust the color here or change which palette color that area uses, in that area's **Section Colors** controls in the Customizer.
